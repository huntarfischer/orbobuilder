# **Field Theory Astrology**

## **Master Glossary, Version 1.0**

I’m standardizing the project term as **synchronic synastry**. “Synchronic” describes a meaningful relation to the present moment; “synchronous” would mean only that two calculations occur at the same clock time.

### **The five-chart distinction**

**Natal chart:** what a person carries.

**Moment chart:** what time supplies.

**Synchronic composite:** what emerges when that person encounters that moment.

**Relationship composite:** what emerges between two people.

**Synchronic synastry:** how two people’s present-tense fields encounter one another under the same sky.

That is the central grammar of the system.

# **I. Foundational Concepts**

### **Field Theory Astrology**

**Core term.** An astrological framework in which a chart does not operate only as a fixed description of an individual. Meaning emerges through fields created between people, times, places, relationships, institutions, and events.

The natal chart supplies enduring structure, but lived experience occurs through the native’s encounters with other structures.

### **Field**

**Core term.** A relational environment generated by the interaction of two or more astrological structures. A field is not reducible to either participant, just as a relationship is not reducible to either person within it.

A field has its own organization, emphasis, momentum, and history.

### **Field Participant**

Any person, moment, place, institution, relationship, event, or symbolic object contributing astrological structure to a field.

### **Native**

The person whose natal chart provides the constitutional reference point for an analysis.

### **Natal Constitution**

The relatively stable pattern carried by the native from birth. It describes the native’s underlying planetary functions, houses, rulerships, sect, and structural predispositions.

A synchronic placement changes through time; the natal function producing it remains constitutionally rooted.

### **Moment**

A particular temporal environment represented by the astronomical sky at a specific instant.

### **Moment Chart**

The chart of a moment before it has been related to a native or another field participant. It is an environmental input, not yet a native-specific placement.

### **Field Encounter**

The meeting of two astrological structures that produces a third, derived structure.

Examples include:

* person \+ person  
* person \+ moment  
* relationship \+ moment  
* person \+ election  
* two person-moment fields \+ one another

### **Emergence**

The appearance of qualities at the field level that cannot be fully explained by reading the contributing charts separately.

A composite Venus, for example, is not simply Person A’s Venus plus Person B’s Venus. It is the Venusian function of the relationship that exists between them.

### **Relational Field**

A field generated between two or more people. Its simplest formal representation is the relationship composite, although its lived expression also includes synastry, chronology, transits, and each participant’s unequal relationship to the composite.

### **Composite Field**

The emergent astrological structure represented by a composite chart.

“Composite chart” refers to the calculated chart. “Composite field” refers to the living relational phenomenon represented by that chart.

### **Field State**

The complete condition of a field at one specified moment, including placements, houses, aspects, dispositors, chains, phase states, lots, and active timing conditions.

### **Field Event**

A meaningful change in field state, such as:

* a sign or house ingress  
* an aspect entering orb  
* an aspect perfecting  
* a phase flip  
* a dispositor change  
* a mutual reception beginning or ending  
* a terminal keeper changing  
* multiple timing systems concentrating on one period

### **Field Signature**

A recurring configuration that gives a field its recognizable character across time.

A signature may involve repeating planets, signs, houses, aspect families, dispositor chains, lots, or phase relationships.

### **Field Activation**

The process by which a latent natal or composite pattern becomes emphasized through contact with another chart, a timing system, or a present moment.

### **Resonance**

A correspondence between field structures that increases their symbolic salience. Resonance may occur through aspect, repetition, rulership, degree, house topic, timing, or shared dispositorship.

Resonance does not necessarily imply ease. A square can resonate as powerfully as a trine.

### **Story-Mass**

**Working theoretical term.** The accumulated symbolic, emotional, historical, and narrative weight carried by a field.

A relationship with years of shared events possesses more story-mass than a brief encounter, even when both produce mathematically valid charts. Story-mass helps explain why formally similar configurations can manifest with dramatically different force.

### **Asymmetrical Impact**

**Core interpretive principle.** A shared field does not affect all participants equally.

The relationship composite may be one chart, but each person encounters that chart through a different natal structure, biography, role, level of investment, and capacity to act. One participant may embody the field publicly while another carries it privately or somatically.

### **Field Asymmetry**

The broader condition producing asymmetrical impact. It includes differences in natal receptivity, house overlays, rulership, timing, power, social position, story-mass, and conscious participation.

### **Constitutional Meaning**

What a planet or configuration means as part of the native’s enduring natal structure.

### **Present-Tense Meaning**

What that planetary function is currently doing within the native-moment field.

This distinction is foundational: natal Mars describes the constitutional Martian function; synchronic Mars describes the current location and condition of that function.

# **II. Chart Objects and Comparison Modes**

### **Natal Chart**

The astronomical chart of birth. In Field Theory Astrology, it acts as the native’s stable constitutional matrix.

### **Natal Synastry**

The aspects between two natal charts. It describes enduring inter-chart relationships between the participants’ constitutional structures.

### **Overlay**

Two charts displayed together without calculating aspects between them.

An overlay is especially useful for seeing which houses of one chart receive the planets or points of another. It answers **where one chart enters another**, rather than **how its planets aspect the other chart**.

### **Synastry**

The systematic comparison of aspects between two charts. Either chart may be natal, composite, progressed, synchronic, or moment-based, provided the chart types are clearly named.

### **Relationship Composite**

A chart formed by calculating the midpoints of corresponding factors in two natal charts.

It represents the emergent field of the relationship rather than either person individually.

### **Natal Composite**

Another name for a relationship composite created from two natal charts. This term is especially useful when distinguishing it from synchronic composites.

### **Composite Overlay**

An overlay involving one or more composite charts.

Examples include:

* natal chart \+ relationship composite  
* two relationship composites  
* natal chart \+ synchronic composite

### **Composite Synastry**

Synastry involving at least one composite chart.

The term is broad and must be qualified. It could mean:

* natal chart to relationship composite  
* relationship composite to relationship composite  
* synchronic composite to synchronic composite

The final case is more precisely called **synchronic synastry**.

### **Transits to the Composite**

The aspects made by the current sky to a pre-existing relationship composite.

This describes how the sky activates the relationship field. It is not the same as creating a person-moment midpoint chart.

### **Three-Way Composite**

A composite produced by midpointing three participants or chart objects into one derived chart.

Synchronic synastry is not a three-way composite. It preserves two distinct person-moment fields and compares them rather than averaging them into one object.

### **Composite Framing**

The broader system for placing, displaying, comparing, timing, and interpreting composite-derived charts.

It includes overlays, composite synastry, synchronic composites, transits to composites, house perspectives, dispositorship, chronology, and playback.

### **Frame Protocol**

The rule used to generate comparable field states.

Examples include:

* every day at local noon  
* every hour  
* every day at the native’s birth time  
* every exact aspect event  
* every sign or phase transition

A chronology is only internally coherent when its frames use a stable protocol.

# **III. Synchronic Composite Astrology**

### **Synchronic Composite**

**Core coined term.** The derived chart formed by midpointing a natal chart with a moment chart, factor by factor.

SCA(t)=C(A,Mt)

It represents the field created between a particular native and a particular moment.

### **Native-Moment Field**

The living field represented by a synchronic composite.

The chart is the calculated representation. The native-moment field is the phenomenon represented by it.

### **Synchronic Placement**

**Core coined term.** The present-tense field location of a natal planetary function.

For planet PPP:

SP(t)=midpoint⁡(NP,TP(t))

The natal placement belongs to the native. The transiting placement belongs to the sky. The synchronic placement belongs to the encounter between them.

### **Transit Input**

The transiting position used to generate a synchronic placement.

A transit is not itself a placement belonging to the native. It is one term in the native-moment calculation.

### **Synchronic Function**

The planetary function as it currently operates inside the native-moment field.

“Synchronic Mars” therefore means the native’s Martian function as fielded by the present moment, not transiting Mars considered alone.

### **Composite Frame**

One sampled synchronic composite generated at a specific time under a declared frame protocol.

### **Composite Chronology**

An ordered sequence of synchronic composite frames:

CCA​={SCA​(t1​),SCA​(t2​),…,SCA​(tn​)}

It shows the native-moment field evolving through time rather than presenting a single frozen chart.

### **Field-State Engine**

A system that calculates, stores, indexes, compares, and plays back changing field states.

Orbo Timespine is therefore more than a chart calculator. It is a temporal field-state engine.

### **Fixed Spatial Origin**

The geographical location at which moment charts are calculated for a chronology.

The current working default is the native’s birthplace, which preserves one spatial reference while time changes.

### **Field Time**

The absolute instant used to calculate a field state.

### **Frame Time**

The recurring civil-time rule used to select field times, such as birthplace local noon each day.

### **Synchronic Return**

A frame in which a synchronic placement returns to a prior displayed position, axis state, phase state, sign, house, or complete configuration.

The exact meaning must be qualified because a displayed-point return is not necessarily a full phase return.

# **IV. Midpoint Geometry and Phase Mechanics**

### **Midpoint**

The point halfway between two zodiacal longitudes.

Because the zodiac is circular, every midpoint also possesses an antipodal counterpart 180° away.

### **Minor-Arc Midpoint**

The midpoint located along the shorter arc between two positions. This is the familiar displayed point used by most composite chart systems.

### **Antipodal Pair**

The two possible midpoint endpoints separated by 180°.

The pair is the complete midpoint object. Choosing only one endpoint conceals half of its geometry.

### **Midpoint Ambiguity**

The inherent 180° ambiguity created when two positions are midpointed on a circle.

This is not an error in calculation. It is a property of circular midpoint geometry.

### **Axis State**

The underlying 180° midpoint object before one endpoint has been selected for display.

### **Axial Coordinate**

The midpoint’s position measured modulo 180°. It identifies the axis without deciding which antipodal endpoint is currently displayed.

### **Axial-First Model**

The computational principle that the system should store the midpoint axis as the canonical object and derive the displayed zodiac point from it.

This preserves information that would be lost in a point-only model.

### **Point View**

The familiar 360° zodiacal presentation of one endpoint of an axial object.

### **Phase Bit**

A binary state, conventionally 0 or 1, that determines which endpoint of a midpoint axis is displayed.

### **Phase State**

The complete current endpoint condition of a synchronic placement.

### **Phase Parity**

The relationship between the phase bits of two synchronic placements.

* **Same parity:** the two placements display corresponding endpoints.  
* **Opposite parity:** one placement displays the antipodal endpoint relative to the other.

### **Flip**

A change from one phase endpoint to the other.

A flip occurs when the accumulated unwrapped transit completes another 360° relative cycle, causing the synchronic midpoint’s phase parity to change. The underlying axis remains continuous even when the displayed point changes by 180°.

### **Flip Boundary**

The exact point at which a phase bit changes.

### **Flip Event**

A recorded temporal event marking a flip, including its time, planet, prior phase, new phase, and resulting sign, house, aspect, or dispositor changes.

### **Unwrapped Longitude**

A continuous planetary longitude that is allowed to move beyond 360° or below 0° rather than resetting at the zodiac boundary.

For example, 359°, 361°, and 365° preserve continuous motion more clearly than 359°, 1°, and 5°.

### **Lifted Synchronic Longitude**

The synchronic midpoint calculated in unwrapped space. It allows velocity, retrogradation, returns, and flips to be tracked without artificial zodiacal jumps.

### **Displayed Longitude**

The ordinary 0°–360° zodiacal position shown to the user after axis and phase have been resolved.

### **Synchronic Speed**

The rate at which a synchronic placement moves.

For same-planet natal-to-moment composites, its instantaneous speed is half the transiting planet’s longitudinal speed.

### **Phase Duration**

The time required for a synchronic placement to travel through one complete 180° phase arc.

Its duration depends on the transiting planet’s full accumulated cycle, including retrograde movement.

### **Nested Clocks**

The collection of planetary phase cycles operating simultaneously at different speeds.

The Moon generates a fast clock, the Sun an annual clock, and slower planets produce clocks lasting years or decades. The field is therefore a clockwork of overlapping temporal scales.

### **Retrograde Reversal**

A period in which a transiting planet’s reversed motion causes the corresponding synchronic placement to reverse at half the transiting rate.

### **Station**

A point at which a synchronic placement’s motion approaches zero because the transiting planet is stationing.

# **V. Houses, Topics, and Perspective**

### **Natal Whole-Sign Anchor**

The project’s primary house policy: all synchronic placements are assigned to the native’s natal whole-sign houses, anchored to the natal Ascendant sign.

The field changes position within the native’s topical architecture; the architecture itself does not move.

### **Topical Continuity**

The interpretive stability created by retaining the same natal whole-sign house framework across every synchronic frame.

For a Scorpio-rising native, Taurus remains the seventh house in every frame.

### **Field House**

The natal whole-sign house occupied by a synchronic placement.

It identifies the life topic through which that present-tense planetary function is operating.

### **Moment House**

A house defined by the actual moment chart or by derived moment/composite angles.

This offers a different perspective from the field house and should be explicitly labeled rather than blended with it.

### **Houses Defined by the Field**

House meanings anchored to the native or relationship whose field is being studied.

### **Houses Defined by the Moment**

House meanings derived from the horizon and angles of the temporal event itself.

The two views answer different questions:

* **Field houses:** Where is this happening in the native’s life?  
* **Moment houses:** How is this moment itself spatially organized?

### **Synchronic House Ingress**

The moment a synchronic placement enters a new natal whole-sign house.

This marks a change of topic, even when the underlying natal planet remains constitutionally associated with its natal house.

### **Topical Routing**

The way a planetary concern is redirected through the house occupied by its dispositor.

For example, a synchronic Mars in the eighth house disposed by a synchronic Mercury in the sixth routes an eighth-house concern through sixth-house labor, health, service, or maintenance.

### **Topical Alignment**

In synchronic synastry, the degree to which two people’s primary synchronic placements occupy related or compatible natal house topics.

# **VI. Rulership and Field Governance**

### **Immediate Dispositor**

The ruler of the sign occupied by a synchronic placement.

A synchronic planet is disposed by another synchronic planet, not by the natal or transiting planet considered separately.

### **Dispositor Chain**

The sequence created by following each synchronic planet to the ruler of its sign and then following that ruler onward.

It describes how one field concern answers to another.

### **Dispositor Graph**

The complete network of dispositorship relationships within one field frame.

### **Self-Ruled Planet**

A planet occupying one of its own domicile signs, causing its dispositor chain to terminate in itself.

### **Mutual Reception**

A two-planet dispositorship loop in which each planet occupies a sign ruled by the other.

### **Dispositor Loop**

A circular chain involving two or more planets. Mutual reception is the simplest form.

### **Terminal Keeper**

The final planet or closed dispositor loop reached by following a placement’s chain.

The terminal keeper identifies the ultimate governing function to which a current concern answers.

### **Keeper of the Light**

**Working specialized term.** The terminal keeper associated specifically with the native’s sect light or with the dispositorship chain governing that light in a given frame.

It is not automatically synonymous with the terminal keeper of every planet. The concept joins sect, luminary condition, and field governance.

### **Sect Light**

The luminary belonging to the native’s sect:

* Sun in a day chart  
* Moon in a night chart

Within composite framing, the sect light remains a privileged organizing function even as its synchronic placement, dispositor, chain, and keeper change.

### **Chain Convergence**

The condition in which different synchronic placements eventually answer to the same terminal keeper or dispositorship loop.

### **Chain Alignment**

In synchronic synastry, the degree to which two people’s dispositorship paths converge, intersect, or terminate under the same governors.

### **Rulership Alignment**

The degree to which two people’s present-tense concerns answer to the same ruler, compatible rulers, or rulers currently in meaningful aspect.

### **Governance Change**

A field event in which a placement changes sign and therefore changes immediate dispositor, chain, terminal keeper, or all three.

# **VII. Synchronic Synastry**

### **Synchronic Synastry**

**Core coined term.** The synastry between two synchronic composites calculated against the same moment:

SSA,B​(t)=S(SCA​(t),SCB​(t))

It compares how two natives are each being fielded by the same time.

It is not natal synastry, transits to a relationship composite, or a three-way composite.

### **Shared Moment**

The single temporal chart used to generate both participants’ synchronic composites.

The moment is shared, but each participant produces a different field because the natal inputs differ.

### **Two-Field Comparison**

A comparison that preserves each person’s synchronic composite as a separate object rather than merging the participants into one chart.

### **Same-Planet Synchronic Relation**

A comparison between the same synchronic planetary function in two people, such as:

* Moon to Moon  
* Mars to Mars  
* Venus to Venus  
* Ascendant ruler to Ascendant ruler

### **Shared-Sky Cancellation**

The mathematical cancellation that occurs in same-planet synchronic synastry because both people’s placements contain the same transiting planet at the same moment.

This leaves a stable structural separation equal to half the corresponding natal separation.

### **Structural Backbone**

The time-stable geometric relationship underlying a same-planet synchronic pair.

The displayed mode can change through phase flips, but the fundamental aspect family remains fixed.

### **Locked Aspect**

A shorthand for a same-planet relationship whose structural aspect family is fixed.

“Locked” does not necessarily mean that the displayed separation never changes. It means that it can only alternate within its mathematically determined complementary family.

### **Natal Separation**

The angular distance between two people’s natal placements of the same planet.

### **Half-Natal Separation**

The structural separation produced in same-planet synchronic synastry:

δsynchronic​=2δnatal​​

subject to the complementary axial mode.

### **Complementary Aspect Family**

The pair of possible displayed separations produced by one same-planet structural relation:

For example, a natal Moon separation of 116° produces a synchronic family of 58° and 122°, close to a sextile/trine pair.

### **Current Aspect Mode**

The member of the complementary aspect family currently displayed.

The mode is selected by the participants’ phase parity.

### **Mode Shift**

A change from one complementary aspect mode to the other caused by one participant flipping before the other.

### **Phase-Parity Rhythm**

The repeating relational rhythm created as the phase bits of two same-planet synchronic placements move between matching and opposing parity.

### **Persistently Harmonious Family**

A structural family in which both complementary modes fall near harmonious aspects, such as sextile and trine.

### **Persistently Tensional Family**

A family in which both modes remain close to challenging or friction-producing angular relationships.

### **Mixed Aspect Family**

A family whose two complementary modes carry noticeably different interpretive qualities.

### **Cross-Planet Synchronic Relation**

A comparison between different synchronic planetary functions, such as:

* Person A’s Moon to Person B’s Mars  
* Person A’s Ascendant ruler to Person B’s Moon  
* Person A’s Venus to Person B’s Saturn

### **Cross-Planet Dynamics**

The changing geometry of cross-planet synchronic relations.

Unlike same-planet relations, the shared moment does not cancel because two different transiting planets are involved. These aspects can form, perfect, separate, retrograde, disappear, and return dynamically.

### **Primary Spine**

The principal relational structure used to organize synchronic synastry interpretation.

The current model prioritizes:

* ruler to ruler  
* Moon to Moon  
* ruler to Moon  
* Moon to ruler

### **Ruler-to-Ruler Relation, RR**

The aspect between the two natives’ synchronic natal Ascendant rulers.

It compares the present-tense condition of each person’s principal agent or field governor.

### **Moon-to-Moon Relation, MM**

The relationship between the participants’ synchronic Moons.

It describes the emotional, bodily, habitual, receptive, and temporal coordination of the two fields.

### **Ruler-to-Moon Relation, RM**

Person A’s synchronic Ascendant ruler compared with Person B’s synchronic Moon.

It describes how A’s agency meets B’s receptivity and lived immediacy.

### **Moon-to-Ruler Relation, MR**

Person A’s synchronic Moon compared with Person B’s synchronic Ascendant ruler.

Because the relationship may be asymmetrical, RM and MR must not be treated as interchangeable.

### **Aspect Alignment**

The angular coherence of the primary spine and any selected secondary bodies, usually weighted by aspect type and orb.

### **Topical Alignment**

The degree to which the participants’ primary synchronic placements activate related life topics.

### **Rulership Alignment**

The compatibility or convergence of the rulers currently governing each person’s concerns.

### **Chain Alignment**

The degree to which the two fields’ dispositor chains intersect or terminate together.

### **Flip Alignment**

The temporal proximity of significant phase changes in the two fields.

Two people whose Moons or Ascendant rulers are approaching flips together may be entering major field transitions at nearly the same time.

### **Total Alignment**

A composite metric combining aspect, topic, rulership, chain, and flip alignment.

The present working weighting gives aspect alignment the greatest influence while retaining the deeper governance and timing layers.

### **Alignment Window**

A bounded period during which a synchronic synastry relationship remains within a chosen orb or scoring threshold.

### **Relational Timing Event**

An exact or threshold-crossing event in synchronic synastry, including:

* aspect ingress  
* perfection  
* egress  
* mode shift  
* simultaneous flip  
* chain convergence  
* terminal keeper change  
* topical synchronization

### **Field Coherence**

The degree to which two present-tense fields organize around compatible planets, houses, rulers, chains, and timing phases.

Coherence does not necessarily mean comfort. Two people may be coherently organized around conflict.

### **Field Dissonance**

A condition in which the participants’ field structures pull toward incompatible topics, governors, rhythms, or modes.

### **Temporal Coherence**

The degree to which the two fields change phase, topic, rulership, or emphasis together.

### **Relational Asymmetry**

The unequal expression of a shared synchronic synastry event.

For example, a ruler-to-Moon contact may describe one person acting and the other receiving. Reversing the direction changes the relational sentence.

# **VIII. Aspect Timing and Orb Mechanics**

### **Orb**

The permitted angular distance from exact aspect.

### **Orb Window**

The complete interval during which an aspect remains active under a selected orb policy.

### **Aspect Ingress**

The moment an aspect first enters its permitted orb.

### **Aspect Perfection**

The moment the actual separation most closely equals the exact aspect angle.

### **Aspect Egress**

The moment an aspect leaves its permitted orb.

### **Applying Aspect**

An aspect moving toward perfection.

### **Separating Aspect**

An aspect moving away from perfection.

### **Fixed-by-Aspect Orb**

An orb determined solely by aspect type.

### **Weighted-by-Bodies Orb**

An orb adjusted according to the bodies involved, allowing luminaries, angles, or chart rulers greater emphasis.

This is the preferred default for synchronic synastry.

### **Velocity-Scaled Orb**

An orb adjusted according to relative planetary speed.

It is especially useful for event research and for distinguishing brief contacts from long atmospheric alignments.

### **Relative Angular Speed**

The rate at which the separation between two placements changes.

### **Exact-Time Refinement**

The numerical process used to determine precise ingress, perfection, egress, station, or flip times after a coarse chronology identifies the event window.

### **Re-Formation**

The return of an aspect after separation, commonly caused by retrogradation or unequal phase movement.

# **XII. Canonical Distinctions**

### **Transit vs. Synchronic Placement**

A **transit** is what the sky is doing.

A **synchronic placement** is where one of the native’s functions currently lives in the field created with that sky.

### **Composite vs. Synastry**

A **composite** creates a new field object.

**Synastry** compares two objects while preserving their distinction.

### **Relationship Composite vs. Synchronic Composite**

A **relationship composite** is person \+ person.

A **synchronic composite** is person \+ moment.

### **Transits to Composite vs. Synchronic Synastry**

**Transits to a composite** ask how the sky is affecting an existing relationship field.

**Synchronic synastry** asks how two people’s separate person-time fields are relating now.

### **Aspect Family vs. Aspect Mode**

The **aspect family** is the stable axial geometry.

The **aspect mode** is the currently displayed member of that family.

### **Field House vs. Moment House**

The **field house** describes where something occurs in the native’s life.

The **moment house** describes how the event itself is organized around its own horizon.

### **Terminal Keeper vs. Keeper of the Light**

A **terminal keeper** ends any dispositor chain.

The **keeper of the light** ends the chain specifically associated with the sect light or luminary-centered field organization.

### **Structural Harmony vs. Lived Ease**

A mathematically harmonious aspect family does not guarantee an easy lived relationship. Story-mass, natal receptivity, house topics, timing, power, and asymmetrical impact determine how that geometry becomes experience.

## **The project’s central proposition**

**A person is not merely acted upon by transits. Each moment forms a new relational field with the natal chart. Two people therefore do not only possess natal synastry or a shared composite. At every moment, they also possess two changing native-moment fields whose relationship can be calculated, timed, compared, and watched as it moves.**

That final object is **synchronic synastry**. It is the relational weather between two evolving fields, but beneath that weather lies a stranger clock: stable aspect families, alternating phase modes, unequal impact, converging chains, and the accumulated gravity of story.
