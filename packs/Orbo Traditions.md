## **First, these are not really six “schools”**

They are six authors or textual traditions spanning roughly a millennium. Each preserves a different slice of the astrological machine. Selecting one author as a global default would quietly change several unrelated things at once: bounds, triplicity rulers, lots, house treatment, aspect doctrine, timing methods, and even the philosophy of why astrology works.

The more accurate Orbo architecture is **one default profile assembled from several traditions**, with each component carrying its source provenance.

## **The six traditions**

| Tradition | Basic character | Strongest contribution | Limitation as a universal default |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Dorotheus** | Practical judgment | Sect, triplicity rulers, topics, elections | Less systematic as a computational framework |
| **Ptolemy** | Natural philosophy | Astronomical causation, light, phase, geometry | Deliberately excludes or minimizes many common techniques |
| **Valens** | Working astrologer’s laboratory | Lots, time-lords, profections, releasing, examples | Dense, inconsistent in places, preserves competing systems |
| **Firmicus** | Late-antique encyclopedia | Detailed planet/sign/house combinations | Extremely literal, deterministic, and socially antiquated |
| **Paulus** | Concise technical handbook | Definitions, taxonomy, standardized rules | More reference manual than interpretive method |
| **al-Bīrūnī** | Mathematical and comparative encyclopedia | Calculations, variant doctrines, enormous lot catalog | Records systems more than he constructs one unified system |

### **Dorotheus: the judge**

Dorotheus asks, essentially, **“What does this configuration promise, and how well can it deliver?”** His method is strongly organized around sect, angularity, planetary condition, and especially the triplicity rulers. He uses the triplicity lords not merely as dignity points, but as successive carriers of support, fortune, endurance, and life development. His examples often read the signs as topical places while separately noticing the power of the cardines or angles.

For Orbo, Dorotheus is excellent for:

* judging whether a placement has support  
* dividing testimony across phases of life  
* electional logic  
* producing coherent topic-based interpretations

His astrology feels architectural. A planet is not merely “strong”; it is supported, witnessed, protected, obstructed, or abandoned within a larger arrangement.

### **Ptolemy: the physicist**

Ptolemy is the closest fit for your **field ontology**. He tries to ground astrology in observable celestial relationships: heat, cold, moisture, dryness, solar phase, seasonal position, angular distance, visibility, horizon position, and geometrical aspect. His planets do not merely carry mythic meanings. Their powers change through their relationship to the Sun, the atmosphere, the seasons, and the observer’s local horizon.

That gives Orbo a powerful conceptual foundation:

> Astrology is the measurement of a changing celestial field whose bodies acquire different expressions through light, phase, geometry, motion, and location.

But Ptolemy is a poor **sole technical default**. He pares away techniques he considers insufficiently natural or demonstrable. He uses relatively few lots, treats only the Lot of Fortune prominently, and calculates Fortune in the same direction for day and night charts.

Ptolemy should govern Orbo’s explanation of **why the field behaves as it does**, not determine every calculation inside it.

### **Valens: the temporal engineer**

Valens is the working astrologer’s workshop, crowded with charts, lots, rulers, distributions, profections, circumambulations, releasing techniques, and competing calculations. His *Anthologies* are the largest surviving practical record of Hellenistic astrology, compiled from actual practice rather than reduced into one polished philosophical system.

Valens repeatedly asks:

> Which part of the chart has become active now?

That makes him the natural core of Orbo’s **Timespine**. Valens gives you a chart that unfolds, hands power from one ruler to another, activates lots, and changes its operative center through time.

For Orbo, Valens is strongest for:

* Zodiacal Releasing  
* annual profections  
* distributions and circumambulations  
* lots as distinct channels of life  
* nested time-lord periods  
* comparing natal promise with temporal activation

Valens supplies the gears and escapement. Ptolemy describes the light moving across the clock face.

### **Firmicus: the enormous interpretive archive**

Firmicus preserves a vast Latin synthesis containing signs, houses, aspects, planetary combinations, Fortune, angularity, antiscia, rulers of the nativity, and highly specific delineations. The *Mathesis* is built as an encyclopedic interpretive storehouse, and its opening argument is strongly deterministic.

Firmicus is useful when Orbo needs:

* a large historical delineation corpus  
* detailed planet-in-house and planet-in-sign combinations  
* antiscia and counter-antiscia  
* examples of late-antique synthesis

But Firmicus should **not** supply the default Orbo voice. His judgments are often absolute, punitive, status-obsessed, and tied to the social world of the late Roman Empire. Use him as an archive whose observations are translated into field relationships, not as a prophet barking verdicts from a marble balcony.

### **Paulus: the grammar book**

Paulus is the cleanest late-Hellenistic technical primer among this group. His *Introductory Matters*, composed in 378, preserves concise definitions of signs, planets, places, lots, dignities, subdivisions, and procedural rules. It is less a grand theory or casebook than a carefully packed instrument chest.

Paulus is ideal for Orbo’s:

* terminology  
* data schema  
* glossary  
* rule definitions  
* calculation labels  
* “why am I seeing this?” explanations

When Orbo says, “This planet is operative because…,” Paulus can provide the grammar behind the sentence.

### **al-Bīrūnī: the librarian-mathematician**

Al-Bīrūnī begins with mathematics and astronomy before reaching judicial astrology. His 1029 work is arranged pedagogically and comparatively, often documenting alternative systems rather than pretending that one doctrine was universally accepted.

He also preserves a sprawling catalog of lots. One surviving list includes 97 commonly used lots, while noting that astrologers kept inventing more for specific questions.

For Orbo, al-Bīrūnī should govern:

* provenance  
* alternate formulas  
* comparative settings  
* calculation documentation  
* historical naming  
* the extended lot library  
* warnings when traditions disagree

He is not the default interpretive voice. He is Orbo’s celestial version-control system.

## **The settings where these traditions genuinely disagree**

### **1\. Triplicity rulers**

**Dorotheus** uses three rulers for each triplicity:

| Triplicity | Day | Night | Participating |
| ----- | ----- | ----- | ----- |
| Fire | Sun | Jupiter | Saturn |
| Earth | Venus | Moon | Mars |
| Air | Saturn | Mercury | Jupiter |
| Water | Venus | Mars | Moon |

Dorotheus actively uses all three, often to describe successive conditions or the sustained support available to a topic.

**Ptolemy** reorganizes them according to his natural and sect-based rationale:

* Fire: Sun by day, Jupiter by night  
* Earth: Venus by day, Moon by night  
* Air: Saturn by day, Mercury by night  
* Water: Mars as primary ruler, with Venus by day and Moon by night as co-rulers

Ptolemy’s arrangement is theoretically elegant, but Dorotheus’s three-ruler system is considerably more useful for actual delineation.

**Orbo default:** Dorothean triplicity rulers.

### **2\. Bounds or terms**

Ptolemy records the commonly used **Egyptian bounds**, criticizes their apparent irregularity, and then presents another table based on a damaged ancient text that became known as the **Ptolemaic bounds**. Dorotheus, Paulus, and Firmicus are associated with the Egyptian system.

The Ptolemaic bounds are more theoretically reconstructed. The Egyptian bounds have the broader practical transmission.

**Orbo default:** Egyptian bounds.  
 **Alternate:** Ptolemaic bounds.

Do not call this a complete “Ptolemy mode.” It is only a bounds selection.

### **3\. Lot of Fortune direction**

The dominant Hellenistic convention reverses the luminary relationship by sect:

* Day: Ascendant \+ Moon − Sun  
* Night: Ascendant \+ Sun − Moon

Ptolemy explicitly uses the Sun-to-Moon distance in both day and night charts.

**Orbo default:** sect-reversed Fortune.  
 **Alternate:** Ptolemaic unreversed Fortune.

Spirit should likewise use the complementary sect-sensitive formula.

### **4\. Number and importance of lots**

* **Ptolemy:** very sparse, chiefly Fortune  
* **Dorotheus:** multiple topical lots  
* **Valens:** lots are major structural and predictive anchors  
* **Paulus:** preserves a systematic Hermetic lot tradition  
* **Firmicus:** preserves additional variants  
* **al-Bīrūnī:** huge comparative catalog

**Orbo default:** the seven planetary or Hermetic lots, plus historically important Valens lots. Place al-Bīrūnī’s larger catalog behind an expanded library rather than flooding the natal chart with 97 glowing pins.

### **5\. Houses and angles**

The cleanest Orbo solution is not “Valens houses” versus “Ptolemy houses.”

Use:

* **whole-sign houses for topics**  
* **Ascendant and Descendant degrees as horizon vectors**  
* **MC and IC degrees as meridian vectors**  
* **angular proximity as an independent intensity measurement**

This preserves the whole-sign chart while acknowledging that a planet close to an actual angle has a different field intensity from another planet elsewhere in the same sign.

That is also unusually compatible with your premise: **the house tells us where the energy expresses; the angle tells us how forcefully it enters observable life.**

## **My recommended Orbo default**

### **Orbo Classical Field**

| Component | Default source |
| ----- | ----- |
| Underlying ontology | **Ptolemy** |
| Topical houses | **Whole sign** |
| Angular strength | Degree-based horizon and meridian overlay |
| Sect | Always active |
| Triplicity rulers | **Dorotheus** |
| Bounds | **Egyptian** |
| Fortune and Spirit | Sect-reversed |
| Core lot framework | **Valens \+ Paulus** |
| Time-lord engine | **Valens** |
| Judgment structure | **Dorotheus** |
| Terminology and rules | **Paulus** |
| Variant formulas and provenance | **al-Bīrūnī** |
| Historical delineation archive | **Firmicus**, opt-in and translated |
| Default aspect model | Whole-sign testimony plus degree-based application, separation, and exactitude |

The clean formulation is:

> **Ptolemaic in physics, Valensian in time, Dorothean in judgment, Paulus in grammar, al-Bīrūnī in scholarship, and Firmicus in the archives.**

I would not put “Valens / Ptolemy / Paulus / Firmicus” into one radio-button menu. That creates a false choice. Orbo should show a default profile, then let advanced users open the clockwork and change **bounds, triplicity, lot formulas, timing doctrine, and angular model independently**.
